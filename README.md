# EE477 Digital Communications

Welcome to the course repository for **EE477: Digital Communications**.

Digital Communications examines how information can be represented, transmitted, and recovered reliably in the presence of uncertainty. Rather than focusing on a particular technology or communication medium, the course develops the fundamental engineering principles that govern modern information systems. These principles apply equally to wired, wireless, optical, networked, autonomous, and AI-enabled systems. 

## Course Purpose

This course provides electrical engineering students with the concepts and tools needed to:

- Understand how information is created, represented, transmitted, and protected.
- Analyze communication-system performance and tradeoffs.
- Evaluate the effects of noise, uncertainty, and design decisions.
- Develop intuition for modern communication, networking, sensing, and autonomous systems. 【2-ef9665】【1-7bd26e】

A central theme throughout the course is that communication systems are understandable engineering systems governed by measurable tradeoffs rather than technological "magic." 

## Course Philosophy

EE477 is not intended to make students specialists in a particular communication technology. Instead, the course emphasizes a small set of enduring concepts that can be applied to unfamiliar systems and new technologies. Students should leave the course able to reason about:

- How information is represented
- How uncertainty affects performance
- How reliability can be improved through design
- The relationships among bandwidth, power, data rate, and noise
- The role of redundancy in reliable communication 

## Core Intellectual Pillars

The course is organized around four major themes:

### 1. Information Representation
How information is converted into forms suitable for transmission and processing.

Topics include:
- Sampling
- Quantization
- Pulse-code modulation
- Symbol representation
- Multiplexing

### 2. Signal Space Representation
Viewing communication systems geometrically through:

- Basis functions
- Orthogonality
- I/Q representations
- Signal constellations
- Modulation schemes
- Euclidean distance

### 3. Noise, Uncertainty, and Detection
Understanding how real-world imperfections affect communication systems:

- Noise
- Probability
- Detection
- Error mechanisms
- Performance metrics

### 4. Reliable Information Transfer
Exploring the fundamental limits of communication:

- Entropy
- Channel capacity
- Error-control coding
- Reliability tradeoffs

## Guiding Question

Throughout the semester we will repeatedly return to a simple question:

> **How can information be transmitted through an imperfect channel while preserving its meaning and usefulness?**

By the end of the course, you should be able to analyze unfamiliar communication systems, understand the tradeoffs that govern their performance, and reason about their behavior using first principles.
